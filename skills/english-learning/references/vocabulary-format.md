## Standard card

Use this layout when teaching a single word or phrase:

```
**word** /IPA/  *part of speech* (register if not neutral)
1-line definition in plain English. (VN: nghĩa tiếng Việt — required.)

Examples:
- Example sentence 1 in natural context.
- Example sentence 2 showing a different sense or collocation.

Collocations: most common partners (verbs/adjectives/prepositions). Gloss any non-obvious ones in Vietnamese.
Forms: related word forms (noun ↔ verb ↔ adjective ↔ adverb), if useful.
Pitfall: anything tricky for a Vietnamese speaker — pronunciation, false friend, common mistake.
```

The **Vietnamese gloss is required** — the user has explicitly asked for meanings to be given in Vietnamese. The example sentences themselves stay in English (so the user practises thinking in English), but the *meaning* must always be anchored in Vietnamese.

Not every field is required every time. Drop what doesn't add value. Aim for 6–10 lines total.

## Field-by-field guidance

### Headword + IPA + part of speech

- Bold the headword.
- IPA in slashes, with stress mark `ˈ` before the stressed syllable: `/ˈʌn.dər.stænd/`.
- Use General American by default; mark UK separately if pronunciations differ meaningfully (*schedule* US /ˈskɛdʒuːl/ vs UK /ˈʃɛdjuːl/).
- Part of speech: *n.* noun, *v.* verb, *adj.* adjective, *adv.* adverb, *prep.* preposition, *phr.* phrase.

### Definition

- Plain English. Use simpler English than the headword would normally appear in.
- One short clause; not a dictionary sentence.
- If a word has multiple senses, give the most common one as the headline; mention secondary senses briefly only if relevant to the user's question.

**Vietnamese gloss policy:**
- **Always include a Vietnamese gloss for the headword.** The user has explicitly asked for meanings in tiếng Việt. Never skip it.
- **Clean mapping** (one Vietnamese word covers it): give that word.
  - *cope* → *xoay sở / chống chọi*
  - *decision* → *quyết định*
  - *frustrated* → *bực bội / bức xúc / khó chịu vì không làm được gì*
- **Imperfect mapping** (Vietnamese word is narrower, broader, or covers different senses): give the closest term **and** explain the gap in one short line.
  - *commitment* → *(VN: cam kết / sự dồn sức cho điều gì đó. Lưu ý: 'cam kết' tiếng Việt thiên về lời hứa trang trọng; 'commitment' tiếng Anh rộng hơn — có thể là dồn sức làm gì lâu dài, hoặc là việc đã hẹn trên lịch.)*
  - *accountable* → *(VN: chịu trách nhiệm / có thể bị truy trách nhiệm. Khác với 'responsible' — 'accountable' nhấn mạnh việc phải giải trình, có hậu quả nếu sai.)*
- **Polysemous words** (multiple distinct senses): number them and gloss each.
  - *paste* (1) hồ, keo dán; (2) bột nhão (như *tomato paste* — sốt cà chua đặc); (3) thủy tinh giả đá quý (trong nữ trang); (4) dán (động từ — dán văn bản đã sao chép).
- **Format options**:
  - Short, single-word equivalent → inline parenthetical: `(VN: quyết định)`
  - Longer explanation → separate line: `nghĩa: …`
  - Multi-sense → numbered list under the English definition
- **Diacritics**: write Vietnamese with proper diacritics (*xoay sở*, not *xoay so*) unless the user is consistently writing without them — then mirror their style.

### Examples

- 2 examples is the default; 3 if the word has clearly distinct uses.
- Each example must be **a full, natural sentence** — not a phrase fragment.
- Vary contexts: don't put both examples in the same situation.
- Highlight the headword in bold inside the example.
- Prefer examples that *also* model a useful collocation.

### Collocations

- Show the patterns the word actually appears in, not the word in isolation.
- Format: bullet of common pattern + 1-line gloss.
- Example for *make*: *make a decision, make a mistake, make progress, make sense, make sure, make time.*

### Forms

- List word-family members when useful: *decide (v.) — decision (n.) — decisive (adj.) — decisively (adv.).*
- Especially useful for advanced learners building productive vocabulary.

### Pitfall

- Optional but valuable. Examples:
- *Pronunciation: /θ/ at the start — don't say "tank you."*
- *Don't confuse with* X (false friend).
- *In Vietnamese you'd say* X *but English speakers say* Y.
- *Often miscapitalized:* always lowercase / always capitalized.

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — common verb (intermediate)

> **cope** /koʊp/ *v.* (often + *with*)
> To deal successfully with a difficult situation. *(VN: xoay sở / chống chọi / vượt qua khó khăn — không có một từ tiếng Việt khớp hoàn toàn; "cope" hàm ý mình **quản lý được** tình huống chứ không chỉ là chịu đựng.)*
>
> Examples:
> - I can't **cope with** this much stress.
> - She's **coping** well after the divorce.
>
> Collocations: *cope with stress / pressure / a workload / a loss / change.*
> Forms: *cope (v.) — coping (adj.: coping mechanism — cơ chế ứng phó; coping strategy — chiến lược ứng phó).*
> Pitfall: pronunciation /koʊp/ — diphthong /oʊ/, then final /-p/ released. Don't drop the final /p/.

### Example 2 — abstract noun (advanced)

> **commitment** /kəˈmɪt.mənt/ *n.*
> (1) A promise or strong intention to keep doing something. (2) A thing you have to do that takes time.
> *(VN: (1) sự cam kết / sự dồn sức cho điều gì đó lâu dài; (2) việc đã hẹn / việc phải làm. Lưu ý: "cam kết" tiếng Việt thiên về lời hứa trang trọng; "commitment" tiếng Anh rộng hơn — có thể là dồn sức làm việc gì lâu dài, hoặc đơn giản là "việc đã có trên lịch.")*
>
> Examples:
> - Marriage is a long-term **commitment**.
> - I have too many work **commitments** this week to come.
>
> Collocations: *make a commitment* (đưa ra cam kết), *keep a commitment* (giữ lời cam kết), *a long-term/short-term commitment*, *prior commitments* (các việc đã có lịch trước).
> Forms: *commit (v.) — committed (adj.) — commitment (n.).*
> Pitfall: don't equate it with *cam kết* alone. *Commitment* can also be neutral scheduling: *I have a commitment at 3pm* = *Tôi có việc lúc 3 giờ.*

### Example 3 — phrasal verb (intermediate)

> **show up** /ʃoʊ ˈʌp/ *phr. v.* (informal)
> To arrive at a place, especially when expected. *(VN: xuất hiện / có mặt / đến (nơi đã hẹn). Khác với "xuất hiện" chung chung — "show up" luôn hàm ý đến một nơi cụ thể, thường là sự kiện mình được mời.)*
>
> Examples:
> - She didn't **show up** to the meeting.
> - He **showed up** late, again.
>
> Collocations: *show up late / on time / unannounced* (đến mà không báo trước) */ drunk*.
> Register: informal — in formal writing prefer *attend* (tham dự) or *arrive* (đến).
> Pitfall: don't translate it as just *xuất hiện* in every case — *show up* always implies physical presence at a specific event.

### Example 4 — adjective (beginner-intermediate)

> **frustrated** /ˈfrʌs.treɪ.t̬ɪd/ *adj.*
> Annoyed because you can't do something you want to do, or because something keeps going wrong. *(VN: bực bội / bức xúc / khó chịu vì không làm được gì hoặc vì mọi thứ cứ trục trặc. Gần với "bực mình" nhưng có thêm sắc thái "bất lực vì cản trở.")*
>
> Examples:
> - I'm **frustrated** because the printer won't work.
> - She got **frustrated** trying to explain.
>
> Collocations: *get frustrated* (trở nên bực bội), *feel frustrated*, *frustrated with* (a person/situation), *frustrated by* (a problem).
> Forms: *frustrate (v. — làm ai đó bực bội) — frustration (n. — sự bực bội) — frustrating (adj., describes the cause: "this is frustrating" = cái này gây bực bội).*
> Pitfall: *frustrated* (mình cảm thấy bực) vs *frustrating* (cái gì đó gây bực cho mình). ❌ *I'm frustrating* (= mình làm người khác bực) → ✅ *I'm frustrated* (= mình đang bực).

## When to deviate from the standard format

- **Just-pronounce questions** (*how do you say X?*) → drop most fields, keep IPA + stress + 1 audio-style example.
- **Translation requests** (*how do you say "có lẽ" in English?*) → list 2–4 candidates with register/use differences (*maybe / perhaps / probably / possibly*), then example sentences.
- **Synonyms/distinctions** (*what's the difference between X and Y?*) → side-by-side comparison, one example each, key contrast bullets.
- **Idioms** → meaning + literal warning (*don't take this literally*) + 2 examples + register note.
- **Multi-sense words** that the user asked about generically → number the senses (1, 2, 3) and give one card per sense, lighter than the standard.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid these — they look thorough but waste tokens and don't help learning:

- **Listing every dictionary sense** when the user asked about one specific use.
- **Translating every example sentence into Vietnamese** — gloss the *meaning* in Vietnamese, but keep example sentences in English so the user practises thinking in English.
- **Etymology** unless it materially aids memory (rare).
- **Pronouncing in Vietnamese script** (*"đọc là khâu-pờ"*) — IPA is more precise; teach the user to read IPA over time.
- **Long preambles** (*"Great question! 'Cope' is a really useful word..."*) — start with the card.
- **Synonym dumps without distinction** (*cope = handle, manage, deal with, tackle*) — these aren't interchangeable; distinguish or omit.
