## Articles (a / an / the)

Vietnamese has no articles. Definiteness is conveyed by context, classifiers (*con*, *cái*, *chiếc*), or demonstratives (*này*, *đó*). Vietnamese learners therefore omit articles, or insert them where they don't belong.

**Common errors:**
- ❌ *I am student.* → ✅ *I am a student.* (singular countable noun needs an article)
- ❌ *I went to school by car.* → ✅ correct as is — *by car*, *by bus* drop articles in fixed expressions
- ❌ *The life is hard.* → ✅ *Life is hard.* (general/abstract noun, no article)
- ❌ *I love music of Mozart.* → ✅ *I love the music of Mozart.* (specific, identified by post-modifier)
- ❌ *Sun is hot.* → ✅ *The sun is hot.* (unique reference)

**Rule of thumb:**
- *a/an* → first mention of a singular countable thing (any one of them)
- *the* → already-known, unique, or specified by context/postmodifier
- ∅ (no article) → plurals and uncountables in general statements (*Dogs bark.* / *Water boils at 100°C.*)

## Plurals and countability

Vietnamese nouns don't change form for number. Number is shown by classifiers (*ba con chó* = three CL dog).

**Common errors:**
- ❌ *I have two book.* → ✅ *I have two books.*
- ❌ *Many peoples* → ✅ *Many people* (*people* is already plural; *peoples* means "ethnic groups")
- ❌ *I need some informations.* → ✅ *I need some information.* (uncountable)
- ❌ *I bought a furniture.* → ✅ *I bought a piece of furniture* / *some furniture*

**Important uncountables** (no plural, no *a/an*): *information, advice, furniture, equipment, news, software, hardware, research, knowledge, progress, work, money, traffic, weather, homework, vocabulary, feedback*

**Tricky countables and uncountables**:
- *experience* (uncountable) = work/life experience; *an experience* (countable) = a specific event
- *time* (uncountable) = duration; *a time* (countable) = an occasion (*the first time*); *times* = occurrences
- *hair* (uncountable, on head); *a hair* (single strand)
- *coffee* (uncountable, the substance); *a coffee* (a cup of)

## Verb tenses

Vietnamese uses time markers (*đã*, *đang*, *sẽ*, *rồi*) but does not inflect the verb. Vietnamese learners frequently use the bare verb form, regardless of time.

**Common errors:**
- ❌ *Yesterday I go to school.* → ✅ *Yesterday I went to school.*
- ❌ *I work here since 2020.* → ✅ *I have worked here since 2020.* / *I've been working here since 2020.* (use present perfect for "since/for + ongoing")
- ❌ *I am knowing him.* → ✅ *I know him.* (state verbs don't take continuous: *know, like, want, believe, understand, own, mean, prefer*)
- ❌ *When I will arrive, I will call.* → ✅ *When I arrive, I will call.* (no future after time conjunctions: *when, before, after, until, as soon as*)
- ❌ *I am here since 9am.* → ✅ *I have been here since 9am.*

**Tense quick map**:
- Past, finished: simple past (*I ate*)
- Past with present relevance / unfinished period: present perfect (*I have eaten today*)
- Action in progress now: present continuous (*I am eating*)
- General truth / habit: simple present (*I eat breakfast every day*)
- Action that started in past, still ongoing: present perfect continuous (*I have been eating for an hour*)
- Future planned: *be going to* / present continuous (*I am meeting him tomorrow*)
- Future spontaneous decision / prediction: *will*

## Subject-verb agreement

Vietnamese has no agreement. Vietnamese learners often forget the third-person -s.

**Common errors:**
- ❌ *He go to work.* → ✅ *He goes to work.*
- ❌ *She don't know.* → ✅ *She doesn't know.*
- ❌ *My friend like coffee.* → ✅ *My friend likes coffee.*
- ❌ *Everyone are happy.* → ✅ *Everyone is happy.* (*everyone, somebody, nobody, each* — singular)
- ❌ *The team are winning.* → US: *the team is winning*; UK: *the team are winning* (collective nouns vary)

**Rule:** in the simple present, with *he / she / it / a singular noun*, add -s/-es to the verb. Negation/question uses *does* (which already carries the -s, so the main verb stays bare): *does not go*, *does she go?*

## Word order

Vietnamese is SVO like English. But several specifics differ.

### Adjective position

Vietnamese: noun + adjective (*con chó đen* = "dog black"). English: adjective + noun.
- ❌ *a car red* → ✅ *a red car*
- ❌ *the book interesting* → ✅ *the interesting book*

### Adverb of degree position

Vietnamese: *rất* (very) + adjective/verb is fine for both. English: *very* only modifies adjectives/adverbs, not verbs directly.
- ❌ *I very like it.* → ✅ *I really like it. / I like it a lot. / I like it very much.*
- ❌ *I very want to go.* → ✅ *I really want to go.*

### Multiple adjectives order

English has a rough order: **opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → purpose → noun** (*a beautiful small old round red Italian wooden dining table*). Vietnamese learners often produce wrong orders.
- ❌ *a leather Italian black bag* → ✅ *a black Italian leather bag*

### Question word order

English inverts auxiliary and subject in questions. Vietnamese keeps statement order with a question particle (*không?* / *à?*).
- ❌ *You are happy?* (only acceptable as informal echo question) → ✅ *Are you happy?*
- ❌ *Where you go?* → ✅ *Where do you go? / Where are you going?*
- ❌ *Why she is sad?* → ✅ *Why is she sad?*

## Adjectives and adverbs

Vietnamese makes less of a formal distinction between adjective and adverb forms.

**Common errors:**
- ❌ *He drives careful.* → ✅ *He drives carefully.* (verb modifier needs adverb)
- ❌ *She speaks English good.* → ✅ *She speaks English well.* (*good* = adj, *well* = adv)
- ❌ *He is very careful drives.* → ✅ *He drives very carefully.*

**Note**: some adjectives function as both (*fast, hard, late, early, daily*). *Hardly* ≠ *hard* — *hardly* means "almost not" (*I hardly slept* = I slept very little).

## Prepositions

Prepositions are notoriously arbitrary. Vietnamese prepositions don't map 1-to-1 to English.

**High-frequency mismatches:**
| Vietnamese | English (depends!) |
|---|---|
| *trong* (in) | *in / inside / within / among* |
| *ở* (at/in) | *at / in / on* |
| *với* (with) | *with / to / for / from* (*talk to*, *wait for*, *different from*) |
| *về* (about) | *about / on / regarding* |
| *cho* (for/to) | *for / to* |

**Common errors:**
- ❌ *I am married with him.* → ✅ *I am married to him.*
- ❌ *I depend of you.* → ✅ *I depend on you.*
- ❌ *I am interested for music.* → ✅ *I am interested in music.*
- ❌ *I listen music.* → ✅ *I listen to music.*
- ❌ *I'm afraid from him.* → ✅ *I'm afraid of him.*
- ❌ *I arrived to the airport.* → ✅ *I arrived at the airport.* (*arrive at* a place; *arrive in* a city/country)
- ❌ *In the morning of Monday* → ✅ *On Monday morning*
- ❌ *In Christmas* → ✅ *At Christmas* (UK) / *On Christmas (Day)*

**Time prepositions**:
- *at* + clock time / festival / point: *at 5pm, at noon, at Christmas, at the moment*
- *on* + day/date: *on Monday, on July 4th, on my birthday*
- *in* + month/year/season/part of day: *in July, in 2026, in summer, in the morning* (but **at** night)

## Pronouns and possessives

Vietnamese pronouns are complex (kinship-based: *anh, em, chị, ông, bà*) and English pronouns feel flat by comparison. Specific issues:

- ❌ *He is my brother. He name is Nam.* → ✅ *His name is Nam.* (possessive determiner)
- ❌ *She love her self.* → ✅ *She loves herself.* (one word)
- ❌ *Me and my friend went...* → ✅ *My friend and I went...* (other person first; subject form *I*)
- ❌ *Between you and I* → ✅ *Between you and me* (after a preposition, object form)
- ❌ *Everybody have their book.* → ✅ *Everybody has their book.* (*everybody* = grammatically singular but pairs with singular *their* in modern English)

## Question formation

### Yes/No questions need *do/does/did* with main verbs

- ❌ *You like coffee?* → ✅ *Do you like coffee?* (acceptable casually as *You like coffee?* but not in writing)
- ❌ *Why you came?* → ✅ *Why did you come?*

### Tag questions

English: statement + tag of opposite polarity, with auxiliary matching: *You like coffee, don't you? / She isn't here, is she?*

Vietnamese learners often produce flat tags or wrong polarity:
- ❌ *You are happy, no?* → ✅ *You are happy, aren't you?*

### Indirect questions: do NOT invert

- ❌ *I don't know where is he.* → ✅ *I don't know where he is.*
- ❌ *Can you tell me what time is it?* → ✅ *Can you tell me what time it is?*

## Negation

Vietnamese uses *không* (not) in front of the verb. English requires an auxiliary.

- ❌ *I not understand.* → ✅ *I don't understand.*
- ❌ *He no come.* → ✅ *He doesn't come.* / *He isn't coming.*
- ❌ *I am not understand.* → ✅ *I don't understand.* (no auxiliary *be* with simple present; use *do*)

**Double negatives are nonstandard in English:**
- ❌ *I don't know nothing.* → ✅ *I don't know anything.* / *I know nothing.*

## Conjunctions and clauses

### Although / but — pick one, not both

In Vietnamese, *tuy ... nhưng* (although ... but) is a paired structure. In English you use only one.

- ❌ *Although it was raining, but I went out.* → ✅ *Although it was raining, I went out.* / *It was raining, but I went out.*

### Because / so — pick one, not both

Same issue with *vì ... nên* (because ... so).
- ❌ *Because I was tired, so I slept.* → ✅ *Because I was tired, I slept.* / *I was tired, so I slept.*

### Relative clauses

- ❌ *The man which lives next door...* → ✅ *The man who lives next door...* (*who* for people, *which* for things, *that* for either)
- ❌ *The book I bought it yesterday...* → ✅ *The book I bought yesterday...* (don't repeat the object pronoun in relative clauses)

## Vocabulary calques and false friends

Direct translation from Vietnamese often produces unnatural English.

| Vietnamese phrase | Wrong calque | Natural English |
|---|---|---|
| *rất thích* | *very like* | *really like* / *like a lot* |
| *ăn cơm* | *eat rice* | *eat (a meal)* / *have lunch/dinner* |
| *đi học* | *go to study* | *go to school* / *study* |
| *học bài* | *study lesson* | *study* / *do homework* / *review* |
| *chơi điện thoại* | *play phone* | *use my phone* / *be on my phone* |
| *mở đèn / tắt đèn* | *open/close the light* | *turn on / turn off the light* |
| *uống thuốc* | *drink medicine* | *take medicine* |
| *mặc áo* | *wear shirt* | *put on a shirt* (action) / *wear a shirt* (state) |
| *nói tiếng Anh* | *say English* | *speak English* |
| *chết rồi* (figurative) | *I'm dead already* | *I'm in trouble* / *I'm done for* |
| *bao nhiêu tuổi?* | *how much age?* | *How old are you?* |
| *anh/em ăn cơm chưa?* | *Have you eaten rice yet?* | *Have you eaten?* (greeting; English doesn't say *rice*) |
| *cảm ơn nhiều* | *thank you many* | *thanks a lot* / *thank you very much* |

**False friends** (similar-looking Vietnamese-derived or French-derived words with different meanings):
- *actually* ≠ *hiện tại* — *actually* means "in fact"; "currently" is *currently / right now / at present*
- *eventually* ≠ *cuối cùng* sometimes — *eventually* = "after a long time / in the end"; not "finally" in the sense of "at last"
- *sympathy* ≠ *sympa* — English *sympathy* = pity; Vietnamese loanword *sympa* (from French *sympathique*) = nice/friendly

## Punctuation and capitalization

- Vietnamese uses lowercase for days, months, languages, nationalities. **English capitalizes them**: *Monday, July, English, Vietnamese*.
- Vietnamese uses spaces around punctuation in some styles. **English does not** put a space *before* a period, comma, question mark, etc.: ❌ *Hello , world .* → ✅ *Hello, world.*
- Capitalize the first word of every sentence and the pronoun *I* always.

## Quick error-spotting checklist

When reviewing user-written text, scan for these in order — they catch the majority of L1-transfer errors:

1. **Articles**: every singular countable noun should have *a/an/the* or a determiner. Plurals/uncountables can be bare.
2. **Verb form**: tense matches the time reference? Third-person singular -s present? Past tense for completed past actions?
3. **Subject-verb agreement**: singular subject → singular verb form?
4. **Word order**: adjective before noun? Question inverted? Adverb (not adjective) modifying the verb?
5. **Preposition collocations**: known traps (*married to, depend on, interested in, listen to, arrive at, afraid of*).
6. **Plurals**: countable plurals marked? No plurals on uncountables?
7. **Although/but, Because/so**: only one in a sentence.
8. **Capitalization**: days, months, languages, nationalities, proper nouns, sentence start, *I*.
