## Verb tenses — the 12 main ones

English encodes time and aspect on the verb. Vietnamese encodes them with separate words (*đã, đang, sẽ, rồi, từng*) and leaves the verb unchanged. This means Vietnamese speakers must learn to *change the verb form*, which feels alien.

### 1. Simple present
- **Use**: habits, general truths, scheduled events.
- **Form**: base verb (+s/-es with *he/she/it*).
- *I work in Hanoi. / She works in Hanoi. / Water boils at 100°C.*
- **VN trap**: forgetting -s (*She work* ❌). Drill: *He goes, she goes, it goes.*

### 2. Present continuous
- **Use**: action happening now; temporary situations; planned future.
- **Form**: *am/is/are + V-ing.*
- *I am writing an email. / He is staying with friends this week. / We are flying to Tokyo tomorrow.*
- **VN trap**: using continuous with state verbs — *I am knowing him* ❌. State verbs (*know, like, want, need, believe, own*) stay simple.

### 3. Simple past
- **Use**: completed action at a definite past time.
- **Form**: V-ed (regular) or irregular form. Negation/question with *did + base verb*.
- *I went to the market yesterday. / Did you call her? / She didn't see me.*
- **VN trap**: keeping the base verb (*Yesterday I go* ❌); using *did* and a past form together (*did went* ❌).

### 4. Past continuous
- **Use**: action in progress at a past moment; background scene.
- **Form**: *was/were + V-ing.*
- *I was sleeping when you called. / At 8pm we were eating dinner.*
- **VN trap**: not pairing it with simple past for the interrupting event.

### 5. Present perfect
- **Use**: action in a time period that is still open (today, this week, ever, never), or past action with present relevance.
- **Form**: *have/has + past participle.*
- *I have eaten already. / She has lived in Saigon since 2018. / Have you ever tried sushi?*
- **VN trap**: using simple past with *since/for*. ❌ *I work here since 2020.* → ✅ *I have worked here since 2020.* In Vietnamese, *từ năm 2020* + present-tense verb is fine; in English, *since* forces present perfect.

### 6. Present perfect continuous
- **Use**: action that started in the past and continues now (or just stopped, with visible result), emphasising duration.
- **Form**: *have/has been + V-ing.*
- *I've been studying English for 5 years. / She's tired because she's been running.*
- **Tip**: prefer this over present perfect for ongoing activities; both *I have lived here for 3 years* and *I have been living here for 3 years* work, but the continuous emphasises the *ongoing* aspect.

### 7. Past perfect
- **Use**: an earlier past action, before another past event.
- **Form**: *had + past participle.*
- *When I arrived, she had already left.*
- **Tip**: use only when needed for clarity — if the order of events is obvious, simple past is enough.

### 8. Past perfect continuous
- **Use**: ongoing past action up to a later past point.
- **Form**: *had been + V-ing.*
- *I had been waiting for an hour when the bus finally came.*

### 9. Simple future (will)
- **Use**: spontaneous decision, prediction, promise, offer.
- **Form**: *will + base verb.*
- *I'll help you. / It will rain tomorrow. / I'll have the soup, please.*

### 10. Be going to
- **Use**: planned/intended future; prediction based on present evidence.
- **Form**: *am/is/are going to + base verb.*
- *I'm going to study tonight. / Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.*
- **Tip**: *will* vs *be going to* is a subtle native-speaker distinction. Plans you've already made → *be going to*. Decisions you make right now → *will*.

### 11. Future continuous
- **Use**: action in progress at a specific future time.
- **Form**: *will be + V-ing.*
- *This time tomorrow I'll be flying to London.*

### 12. Future perfect
- **Use**: action completed before a specific future time.
- **Form**: *will have + past participle.*
- *By 2030, I will have lived here for 10 years.*

### Tense decision shortcuts

When the user is unsure which tense to use, ask:
1. **Is the time finished or still open?** Finished → simple past. Still open → present perfect.
2. **Is the action ongoing now?** Yes → continuous form.
3. **Are two past events involved?** Earlier one → past perfect.
4. **Is it a state verb?** Force simple, not continuous.
5. **Time conjunction (when, before, after, until)?** Don't use *will* in that clause — use simple present.

## Articles in depth

The hardest topic for Vietnamese learners. Vietnamese has *no* article system; definiteness is contextual.

### Decision tree

1. Is the noun **plural** or **uncountable**? Used in a **general** sense?
   - Yes → no article. *Dogs bark. / Water is wet.*
2. Is the noun **specific** — known to both speaker and listener (mentioned before, unique, defined by context, identified by a phrase like *of X*)?
   - Yes → *the.* *The dog you saw. / The sun. / The book on the table.*
3. Otherwise (singular countable, first mention, not specific):
   - Use *a / an.* *I saw a dog yesterday.*

### *a* vs *an*

Based on **sound**, not letter:
- *a university* (starts with /j/), *a one-eyed cat* (starts with /w/)
- *an hour* (silent h), *an honest mistake*

### Zero article in fixed expressions

- *go to school / be at school* (as a student)
- *go to bed / in bed* (sleeping)
- *by car / by bus / by train / on foot*
- *at home / at work / at lunch*
- *next week / last year* (no *the* with these)

### *the* with proper nouns

- **No *the***: most countries (*Vietnam, France, Japan*), continents, cities, streets, single mountains, single islands, languages.
- **With *the***: countries with *Republic / Kingdom / States / Emirates* (*the United States, the UK, the Netherlands*), oceans, seas, rivers, mountain ranges (*the Himalayas, the Mekong*), island groups, hotels, museums (*the Louvre*), newspapers (*the Times*).

## Conditionals

Vietnamese forms conditionals with *nếu / thì*. The *if-clause* + *result-clause* structure exists in both languages, but English distinguishes time and likelihood through tense shifts.

### Type 0 — general truth
- *If/When + simple present, simple present.*
- *If you heat ice, it melts.*

### Type 1 — real future possibility
- *If + simple present, will + base.*
- *If it rains, I'll stay home.*
- **VN trap**: *If it will rain, I'll stay home* ❌ — never *will* in the if-clause.

### Type 2 — hypothetical present/future (unlikely or imaginary)
- *If + simple past, would + base.*
- *If I had a million dollars, I would buy a house.*
- **Note**: *were* (not *was*) for *be* in formal style: *If I were you, I would apologise.*
- **VN trap**: *If I have a million dollars, I will buy* ❌ — for hypothetical, use *had / would*.

### Type 3 — past hypothetical (didn't happen)
- *If + past perfect, would have + past participle.*
- *If I had studied harder, I would have passed.*

### Mixed conditional
- Past condition, present result.
- *If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.*

## Modal verbs

Modals (*can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would, shall, ought to*) express ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice. They:
- Are followed by **bare infinitive** (no *to*): *can go*, not *can to go*.
- Have **no -s** in third person: *she can*, not *she cans*.
- Form questions/negations **without** *do*: *Can she swim?* / *She can't swim.*

| Modal | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| can | ability, informal permission | *I can swim.* |
| could | past ability, polite request, possibility | *Could you help me?* |
| may | formal permission, possibility | *May I come in?* |
| might | possibility (less certain than *may*) | *It might rain.* |
| must | obligation (strong), logical conclusion | *You must wear a helmet. / You must be tired.* |
| have to | external obligation | *I have to work tomorrow.* |
| should | advice, expectation | *You should rest.* |
| ought to | advice (slightly more formal than *should*) | *We ought to leave soon.* |
| would | hypothetical, polite request, past habit | *I would help if I could. / Would you mind...?* |
| shall | offer (UK), formal future (rare) | *Shall I open the window?* |

**VN trap**: using *to* after modals (*I can to swim* ❌). Vietnamese has no equivalent constraint, so this leaks through.

**Negative obligation**:
- *must not* = it's prohibited (*You must not smoke here.*)
- *don't have to* = it's not required (*You don't have to come if you're tired.*)
These are **not** the same. Vietnamese learners often confuse them.

## Passive voice

- **Form**: *be + past participle.*
- *The cake was eaten. / English is spoken in many countries. / She has been promoted.*
- **Use**: when the action's performer is unknown, obvious, unimportant, or you want to focus on the receiver.
- **By + agent**: optional, only include if it adds information.

**VN trap**: Vietnamese uses *được* (positive passive) and *bị* (negative passive). Learners sometimes overuse passive in English where active is more natural.
- ❌ *The book was read by me.* → ✅ *I read the book.* (active is shorter and clearer)

Use passive when:
- The agent is unknown (*My phone was stolen.*)
- The agent is generic (*This temple was built in 1500.*)
- The receiver is the topic (*She was awarded a prize.*)

## Reported (indirect) speech

Backshift the tense one step into the past when the reporting verb is past.

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| "I am tired." | He said (that) he was tired. |
| "I work here." | She said she worked there. |
| "I went home." | He said he had gone home. |
| "I will call." | She said she would call. |
| "I can swim." | He said he could swim. |

Pronouns and time/place references shift too: *yesterday* → *the day before*; *here* → *there*; *tomorrow* → *the next day*.

**Indirect questions don't invert** (also covered in [vietnamese-pitfalls.md](vietnamese-pitfalls.md)):
- ❌ *He asked where am I going.* → ✅ *He asked where I was going.*

## Gerunds vs infinitives

After certain verbs, English uses *V-ing* (gerund). After others, it uses *to + base* (infinitive). Vietnamese has no equivalent distinction — it's pure memorisation, but patterns help.

### Verbs followed by *V-ing*
*enjoy, finish, mind, suggest, recommend, avoid, consider, deny, imagine, miss, practise, give up, keep, postpone, can't help, look forward to, be used to.*

- *I enjoy **swimming**.* (not *to swim*)
- *I look forward to **meeting** you.* (note: *to* here is preposition, not infinitive)

### Verbs followed by *to + V*
*want, need, decide, hope, plan, agree, refuse, promise, manage, learn, offer, fail, pretend, seem, expect, would like.*

- *I want **to leave**.* (not *want leaving*)

### Verbs followed by either, with meaning change
- *stop **to** smoke* = stopped in order to smoke
- *stop **smoking*** = quit smoking
- *remember **to** call* = don't forget; do it
- *remember **calling*** = recall the past act
- *try **to** open* = attempt
- *try **opening*** = test it as a method

### After prepositions: always *V-ing*
- *good at swimming, before leaving, instead of going.* Never *to V*.

## Relative clauses

Define or describe a noun.

| Relative pronoun | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| who | people (subject) | the man **who** lives next door |
| whom | people (object, formal) | the man **whom** I met |
| which | things | the book **which** I bought |
| that | people or things (informal/restrictive) | the man **that** I met |
| whose | possession | the woman **whose** car is red |
| where | place | the town **where** I grew up |
| when | time | the day **when** we met |
| why | reason | the reason **why** he left |

### Defining vs non-defining

- **Defining** (essential to identify): no commas. *The book that I bought is good.*
- **Non-defining** (extra info, removable): commas. *My brother, who lives in Hanoi, is a doctor.* — *that* is **not** allowed in non-defining clauses.

**VN trap**: doubling the object pronoun. ❌ *The book I bought it yesterday is good.* → ✅ *The book I bought yesterday is good.*

## Comparatives and superlatives

- **1 syllable**: + *-er / -est.* *fast → faster → fastest.*
- **2 syllables ending in -y**: change *y → i + er/est.* *happy → happier → happiest.*
- **2+ syllables otherwise**: *more / most.* *beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful.*
- **Irregular**: *good → better → best; bad → worse → worst; far → farther/further → farthest/furthest.*

**Patterns**:
- *X is + comparative + than + Y*: *He is taller than me.* (informal) / *taller than I am* (formal)
- *the + superlative + of/in*: *the tallest in the class, the best of all.*
- *as + adjective + as*: *He is as tall as his brother.* (equality)
- *not as ... as / not so ... as*: *He's not as tall as his brother.*

**VN trap**: doubling — ❌ *more taller, most tallest.* Use one or the other.

## Phrasal verbs — quick orientation

Phrasal verbs = verb + particle (preposition or adverb). Often the meaning is non-literal: *give up* ≠ *give* + *up*.

### Separable vs inseparable

- **Separable** (can split with object): *turn on the light / turn the light on / turn it on.* Pronoun MUST go between: ❌ *turn on it* → ✅ *turn it on.*
- **Inseparable** (don't split): *look after the baby* (not *look the baby after*).

### Common groups

- **Movement**: *come back, go away, get up, sit down, stand up, lie down.*
- **Daily life**: *wake up, get up, get dressed, get ready, come over, hang out.*
- **Communication**: *call back, hang up, speak up, shut up, bring up* (a topic).
- **Problem-solving**: *figure out, work out, sort out, deal with, put up with, get through.*
- **Relationships**: *get along (with), break up, make up, fall out, look up to.*

Teach phrasal verbs in **clusters by particle** (all *-up* meanings, all *-out* meanings) — Vietnamese learners benefit from seeing the pattern in the particle.
